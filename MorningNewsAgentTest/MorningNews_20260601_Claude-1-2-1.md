### 12. FAO: Six-to-Twelve Month Window to Prevent Hormuz-Driven Global Food Crisis 🔴

**Alert:** 🔴
**Summary:** The UN Food and Agriculture Organisation has warned that the Strait of Hormuz closure has opened a six-to-twelve month window within which governments and food industry actors must take urgent action to prevent a full-scale global food price crisis. FAO Chief Economist Máximo Torero stated that tanker traffic through the strait has collapsed by more than 90% from its pre-conflict baseline of approximately 40 daily passages, with 30–35% of global crude oil, 20% of natural gas, and 20–30% of global fertiliser supplies no longer moving. UK Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper described the world as "sleepwalking into a global food crisis." The FFPI reached 130.7 in April 2026, up for the third consecutive month, with the Hormuz disruption explicitly cited as a driver of the April rise — notably through fertiliser supply costs cascading into rice, wheat, and vegetable oil prices.
**Horizon:** Medium-term signal (3–12 months): if Hormuz transit is not substantially restored before India and Brazil begin major fertiliser purchases this month, the FAO projects crop losses in 2026/27 staple harvests across South Asia, East Africa, and the Philippines; the window for avoiding the worst outcomes is measured in weeks.

### 13. Fertiliser Supply Chain Collapse — 20–30% of Global Flow Disrupted 🟡

**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** The Strait of Hormuz closure has disrupted an estimated 20–30% of global fertiliser trade, triggering a structural supply shock that is now cascading through agricultural value chains. Middle East granular urea prices rose 19% in the first week of March; Egyptian urea surged 28%. The Kiel Institute for the World Economy estimates global fertiliser prices are averaging 15–20% higher in the first half of 2026. About 30% of global urea supplies have been affected, prompting planting cutbacks in Thailand, the Philippines, Bangladesh, and Australia. Nitrogen fertiliser production has fallen 20% globally, as natural gas — the primary feedstock — is also restricted by the Hormuz closure. Farmers in key markets are shifting toward less nitrogen-intensive crops or reducing application rates, a decision that will depress yields in the 2026/27 harvest cycle.
**Horizon:** Long-term structural signal (multi-year): even if Hormuz reopens within weeks, fertiliser application decisions already made for the current planting season will constrain 2026/27 harvests, sustaining food price pressure into 2027.
