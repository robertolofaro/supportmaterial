### 1. Cape of Good Hope rerouting hardens into a structural shipping pattern 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** Six months into the Hormuz crisis, container-carrier rerouting via the Cape of Good Hope shows no sign of reversing even as Hormuz diplomacy advances. Of the nine largest container lines, four have fully stopped Hormuz/Gulf service, three run reduced or convoy-escorted service, and all nine have shifted Asia–Europe traffic to the Cape, adding roughly two weeks per voyage leg and surcharges of $600–$1,200 per TEU. **Horizon:** Medium-term — carriers' sunk investment in Cape-route scheduling and surcharge structures means the diversion is likely to persist for months even after any Hormuz reopening, given the lead time needed to reverse network planning.
