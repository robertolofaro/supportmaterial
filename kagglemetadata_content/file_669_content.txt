And this time the dedication did not select my usual round of authors- albeit Rawls, Orwell, Bernays, Packard, Eyal are quoted within the book, along with the Italian Constitution and... Claude Constitution.

I selected someone that I equally liked while in high school, at a time when one of my pastimes was to read and compare Constitutions: Catullus, specifically... <em>"Nil nimium studeo, Caesar, tibi velle placere, nec scire utrum sis albus an ater homo."</em>

Which is quite aligned with the title and content.

The UHF Journey 2026_00 actually had a double duty: summarize concepts within the book on "how" to organize those activities, and presenting the roles and concepts assigned to each team member, and introduce what in the book is better described within e.g. chapter 7: leadership within a "swarming" and purpose-oriented temporary organization.

Because I worked and helped design, improve, reform bureaucracies- but I think now as since forever that bureaucracies are about continuity- and potentially (but with humans is difficult- maybe AI will help) technocratic neutrality that transcends tribes, lobbying, and even corruption.

Not creativity. Not problem solving. Not innovation.

Elements from bureaucracies must be part of those three elements, if you want to obtain any of the three: I am against scapegoats, as I think that those who fail often with a small nudge can be key to finding the solution and avoiding an encore.

And walked the talk as a management consultant: listening more than talking, and having a penchant for listening to those that many avoid in most organizations- people who complain, complain, complain- but based on evidence.

Because they might be disheartened, disappointed, might have develop their cynical side against any change- but, if they talk and share in a structured way, they actually still care about the organization.

The mini-book actually embeds in chapter 4 a primer on using AI in organizations, not just temporary and leaderless.
