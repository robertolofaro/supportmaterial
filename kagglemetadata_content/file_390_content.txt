While the first part of the title is familiar to everybody, and there is at least a partial convergence on its meaning, the second part is still a matter of debate.<br /><br />Few days ago posted a video on my YouTube channel on change, as usual a presentation built as a narrative through bullet lists: [https://robertolofaro.com/ctr-socialimpacts](https://robertolofaro.com/ctr-socialimpacts).<br /><br />The purpose is to inspire thinking, not to provide answers.<br /><br />The key theme across the video is simple: we got used to read on a daily basis on mainstream media about "digital transformation" and "innovation", usually in a blend covering both- and often with a bit of scaremongering.<br /><br />The video actually builds upon previous videos posted on my YouTube channel on change since early 2019, [https://robertolofaro.com/ctr-channel](https://robertolofaro.com/ctr-channel).<br /><br />Being just a dozen minutes long, and mainly built as a bullet list through a roadmap of points that are actually more questions than assertions, obviously most of the points will raise more doubts than offering solutions.<br /><br />Maybe eventually will add more material on each section, but, for the time being, I wanted this video to close close the circle on my 2019 videos on change.<br /><br />In reality, I think that, as I wrote often in the past, change has to be seen within the overall context where change is applied- a systemic context.<br /><br />And this all start with... understanding where you are, either before or after you have a rough idea of where you want to be heading to.<br /><br />The most puzzling element in many change initiatives that I observed or studied (I tried to avoid it in those I was part of or leading), both on the micro-economic and macro-economic level is how often they sound as if designed by a big architect... playing with Lego(tm) bricks.<br /><br />In my first "digitalization" projects in the 1980s, sometimes offices were receiving their first desktop computers.<br /><br />And, at the time, most of the "digitalization" meant exactly that: convert existing processes into something supported by computers.<br /><br />Digital transformation has an additional element of complexity: it properly applied, there is a significant element of innovation.<br /><br />Specifically, the point is not just to improve efficiency, or even efficacy, but to consider what you need to do, and what you will need to do further down the road.<br /><br />A short narrative through the themes of the video (in <strong>bold</strong> each of the themes, as shown on [YouTube](https://robertolofaro.com/ctr-socialimpacts)):
<dl>
<dt><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><em>1. outlining the basics</em></span></dt>
<dd>there are <strong>implications</strong> within any change, but it has to be considered the current trend: <strong>is it real change?</strong>. Of course, I think that it is.</dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><em>2. understanding where you are</em></span></dt>
<dd>this is certainly worth doig <strong>before you start</strong> and change initiative, and implies that you <strong>know your environment</strong> (something that shouldn't be taken for granted).</dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><em>3. some use indirect methods to implement change</em></span></dt>
<dd>as an <strong>example- changing Europe</strong> has been done since the Treaty of Rome often via a blend of crisis management and crisis generation. But it is correct to use<strong>crises as a facilitator</strong>? Personally, I doubt- it is more a matter of <strong>choices, choices, choices</strong></dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><em><span style="text-decoration: underline;">4. thinking systemically has other implications</span></em></dt>
<dd>A start should be to consider what does mean <strong>participation in the XXI century</strong>, as <strong>transitions</strong> are affected by what is considered "normal"- including importing tension that is generated outside your own control.</dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><em>5. as an example, I consider few cases</em></span></dt>
<dd>About what could come, e.g. <strong>urbanization</strong> and a new concept of <strong>subsidiarity</strong>, but also what is already starting to change, e.g. in <strong>automotive</strong>, <strong>banking</strong>, and <strong>maintenance</strong>.</dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><em><span style="text-decoration: underline;">6. there are few key concepts about transformation that affects the implementation of any change</span></em></dt>
<dd><strong>phasing out</strong> isn't a trivial issue, as temporarily you will have an overlapping between the old and the new, and how that works out depends on how well defined is your <strong>teleology of transitions</strong>, i.e. "why" more than "how" you are implementing change, as well as how do you plan to manage <strong>conflicts of interests</strong>, and how will be managed <strong>cases of continuity seeking&gt;/strong&gt; (as any change affects the business continuity of somebody).</strong></dd>
<dd></dd>
<dt><em><span style="text-decoration: underline;">7. most changes involving digital transformation and innovation will, in effect</span></em></dt>
<dd>alter the <strong>models of interaction</strong>, and specifically the social models considered as your "reference framework". We are moving from the <strong>mid-XX century pyramid</strong>, to what is our recent and current model, the <strong>early XXI century hourglass</strong>. Professional media and commentators often talk about <strong>the techno-enthusiasts model</strong>, but in reality so far the GAFAs (Google Apple Facebook Amazon) of this world are getting closed to <strong>the tech-elitist model</strong>- as if the choice were between addiction and providing addiction, and then replacing with a new form of addiction, not the model enthusiasts often promote.</dd>
</dl>

Each one of the themes obviously deserves more than the roughly 20 seconds of thinking provided to each by the bullet lists- but while I will add more material in the future, you can search within this website to find other articles where I discussed the same themes.<br /><br />
