As I wrote in yesterday's post, while, as Reuters remembered, the latest release was set in motion in July 2026, meanwhile our main ally now routinely calling us "adversarial" just announced that yet another bit of international treaties and consensus since at least decades applies only to others- and announced vaguely the first space deployment.

If you read the announces earlier this week, and even just read the fact sheets, you will notice one point: a different map for each "line" of the initiative.

Which makes sense from a deployment perspective but, from the routine approach to initiatives within the European Union, still requires remembering to all Member States that it is a Union, not a "union &agrave; la carte", as heard in Brussels at a book presentation by a UK diplomat decades ago- with a different majority consensus on each initiative, and, as he presented, UK being the one holding the balance in each.

[Divide et impera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divide_and_conquer) is a game that in Europe, even after the Treaty of Rome, has been routinely played: do you remember when the [Common Agricultural Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Agricultural_Policy) was the largest item on the budget, for political reasons?

It is still relatively supersized, if looking at the actual economic reality of agriculture as part of the European Union economy.

The "defence of the realm" requires a consensus on what is the realm: not a variable geometry, shape-shifting majority- leave that to fighter planes and drones swarms.

In that post yesterday, my note about "vicious papercuts" was not a joke- was a simple representation of reality seen from outside: we issue edicts, announces, etc- but when it turns operational, we hear of French planes, Italian ships, etc.

The USA announces the deployment of weapons in space, credible enough to raise complaints from other countries? 

We present the usual piling up of internal communication channels, hierarchies, overlapping of actors- and what is presented as an operational strategy reads more as a traffic cop in Brussels, having to navigate the traffic priority between all the institutions when there is a traffic jam nearby rond-point Schuman.

A traffic cop trying to ensure that nobody will file a complaint for having been let through in the wrong order- while instead the role of the traffic cop was supposed to be making the traffic flow.

Almost nobody was left out from that long list, but still sounds as a long journey starting with the usual bureaucratic acknowledgment of presence of all the actors.

Today, in one of the new items I read earlier this morning as usual well before breakfast, was actually shared that there will be a meeting at 10am CET followed by a press conference at 12.

If you read those fact sheets, and read since January at least the back-and-forth about the European Commission trying to assert control on defense procurement, and the Member States rebuffing, you can be excused to wonder how all those bullet lists will blend-and-mix, and how many different "&agrave; la carte" majorities will exist within the overall strategic posture.

Again, I do not think that this current Commission showed enough respect of the separation of powers, and therefore probably something or somebody different would be needed to lead.

Still, probably the "entity" representing the Governments of the Member States could generate something more operational, with a clear mandate, and with clear boundaries on who what where- instead of having Member States to play catch up with the latest "ballon d'essai" launched by the President of the Commision, who seems fond to inject integration of Ukraine within any initiative to force an integration with the European Union as a "fait accompli".

Membership in the European Union should be a path of convergence earned, not bestowed "a priori".

So, we will see either today or maybe across few announced meetings over the next few weeks how much we will stay on the old "papermill" (as, while lived in Brussels two decades ago, some called the European Parliament) and more recent "announce-driven" approach, or integration will happen in a domain where integration to achieve operational efficiency and efficacy is a minimum.
