### 1. 2027 Budget law draws record amendments as majority negotiates health, pensions and school measures 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** The 2027 Budget law (legge di bilancio) entered the Chamber with approximately 6,600 amendments, a record. Majority summits chaired by Giorgia Meloni focused on additional resources for the National Health Fund (an extra €500 million discussed), full indexation of lower pensions, and school staffing. Frictions appeared between Fratelli d’Italia and Forza Italia over a measure favouring digital school textbooks; the Lega continued to press on banks’ contribution and a possible “quota 64” pension pathway. The government maintained that every modification requires certified coverage.
**Institutional stage:** Amendments deposited in the Chamber Budget Committee; examination under way; final approval required by 31 December 2026.

### 2. Meloni warns allies that current tensions may force earlier elections 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** In remarks reported by Corriere della Sera, Giorgia Meloni told coalition partners that she would not accept continuing to the natural end of the legislature under present conditions of internal conflict between Lega and Forza Italia. She indicated that, without greater discipline, elections could come in November 2026 rather than November 2027. Senate President Ignazio La Russa has publicly kept open both a spring and an autumn vote. The remarks follow earlier signalling that the timing of the Constitutional Court hearing on the electoral law remains decisive.
**Institutional stage:** Political statement by the Prime Minister; no formal act altering the electoral calendar.

### 3. Campo largo stalls on Ukraine aid and military spending in programme talks 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** Programme tables of the centre-left Campo largo (PD, M5S, Avs and others) failed to close on foreign policy. Giuseppe Conte (M5S) and the red-green alliance continued to insist on limiting or ending arms supplies to Ukraine and on reducing planned military spending; Italia Viva and +Europa resisted a sharp cut. Elly Schlein’s PD sought a mediating formula. Parallel discussions continued on a possible wealth contribution and on the December mobilisation against the budget. The joint commitment to a written programme by the end of November remains formally open.

### 4. Antisemitism bill approved by Chamber; PD votes against and Fiano leaves party 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** The Chamber gave final approval to the bill on the fight against antisemitism. PD, M5S and Avs voted against; Italia Viva and some reformist Democrats voted in favour or abstained. Emanuele Fiano announced his irrevocable departure from the PD after the group’s decision, stating that the no represented an impassable limit for him. Schlein had sought more time for shared amendments; the majority declined. The episode widened the visible divide inside the centre-left on the Israel–Palestine dimension of the text.
**Institutional stage:** Law approved by the Chamber after earlier Senate passage; awaiting promulgation.

### 5. CGIL stages general strike against the budget law 🟡
**Alert:** 🟡
**Summary:** The CGIL called a general strike on 20 November against the 2027 budget measures, citing insufficient resources for health and education and criticising the overall fiscal choices. Demonstrations took place in more than 100 cities. Maurizio Landini stated that if the text is not modified the union would return to protest in front of Parliament from 12 December. UIL did not join; CISL remained more distant. Student and youth groups also mobilised in parallel with European “fight for our future” actions.
**Institutional stage:** Industrial action; no formal parliamentary response yet.
